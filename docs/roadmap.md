## Ecological Considerations
- Species exhibit niche differentiation and habitat specialization
- Ecological succession follows distinct stages:
  - Primary succession: Lichens (pioneer species) → Mosses → Herbaceous plants → Shrubs → Climax community (forest)
- Pioneer species modify abiotic factors, enabling ecological succession for subsequent species
- Static environments lack ecological dynamics
- Modeling this requires environmental heterogeneity and dynamic resource gradients

## Important Notes

- Don't take things for granted!
- A common misconception: while humans have paired chromosomes (diploid), this is not universal in biology
- Many species have 3/4/5 times a chromosome (polyploidy), common in plants and some animals
- Birds and some reptiles/fish have a ZW sex system (ZZ: male, ZW: female)
- Some insects have an X0 sex system (X: male, XX: female)
- Chromosome number and organization vary widely across species - diploidy is just one of many possible configurations
